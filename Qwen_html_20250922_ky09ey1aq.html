✍️ SECTION 1: YOUR INFORMATION (REQUESTER)

💡 STRATEGY: You don’t need to justify WHY you want records — only WHO you are. Never say “for lawsuit.” Say “for public interest reporting.”
⚖️ Legal Citation: 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(3) — “Each agency shall make available to any person...”
→ Example: “John Smith” or “ACLU of Southern California”
→ Tip: Use P.O. Box for anonymity if allowed by state law.
→ STRATEGIC PHRASING: “Please send all correspondence, updates, and records to this email address.”
→ CRITICAL FOR FEE WAIVER: If media/education/nonprofit, state it here to trigger mandatory fee waiver under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(A)(ii)

🏛️ SECTION 2: AGENCY / OFFICE INFORMATION

💡 Pro Tip: Submit to BOTH central FOIA office AND specific bureau. Increases success rate.
⚖️ Procedural Reminder: Federal: 10-day acknowledgment (5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(A)(i)). NY FOIL: 5 business days.
→ Example: “U.S. Department of Homeland Security, ICE”
→ Find on agency FOIA page. Personalizing increases response rate.
→ VERIFY: Some agencies reject requests sent to wrong address.
→ STRATEGIC PHRASING: “I request electronic acknowledgment of receipt within 5 business days per 28 C.F.R. § 16.5(f).”

⚖️ SECTION 3: REQUEST TYPE & LEGAL BASIS

💡 Best Practice: If unsure, cite both federal and state. Example: “Pursuant to 5 U.S.C. § 552 and N.Y. Pub. Off. Law § 87(1).”

📂 SECTION 4: RECORDS REQUESTED — THE HEART

💥 MOST IMPORTANT SECTION — Specificity = success. Vagueness = denial.

A. SUBJECT DETAILS — BE PRECISE

→ BAD: “All NYPD body cam footage” → TOO BROAD → DENIED
→ GOOD: Specific officer, date, time, location, incident
→ Include badge numbers, case IDs, project codes
→ GPS: “40.7580° N, 73.9855° W”
→ Strategic Phrasing: “Include all drafts, edits, versions, and attachments. Include metadata, file properties, and system logs.”

B. RECORD TYPES — CHECK ALL THAT APPLY

💡 Pro Tip: Request “search methodology” — “Please include search terms, databases, and custodians searched.”
→ “All emails between Deputy Chief X and Mayor’s Office re: protest policy Jan 1–31, 2024”
→ “After-Action Report re: Jan 6 Capitol Response, including all drafts”
→ “Minutes of ICE Enforcement Priorities Meeting, Feb 2024”
→ “Contract #XYZ-2023 between DHS and Palantir, including amendments”
→ “NYPD Patrol Guide Chapter 12: Use of Force, including revision history”
→ “All PowerPoint slides used in 2023 ‘Crowd Control’ training for LAPD”
→ “All body cam from Officer #7890, March 15, 2024, 11:30 PM–12:15 AM, 5th Ave & 42nd St”
→ “Excel file ‘2023_Arrests.xlsx’, columns: Date, Charge, Officer ID, Location, Disposition”
→ “OIG Report #2024-01: Inspection of CBP Facility at San Ysidro”
→ “All IA complaints against Officer #12345, 2020–2024, redactions only for personal info per (b)(6)”

C. FORMAT & DELIVERY — FORCE ELECTRONIC

💡 Strategic Phrasing: “Provide in native, machine-readable format. Do not convert spreadsheets to PDF — destroys usability.”

💰 SECTION 5: FEE STATUS — WAIVE OR CAP

💡 Fee Waiver is your RIGHT if media/education/nonprofit
⚖️ Legal Citation: 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(A)(iii) — fee waived if “disclosure likely to contribute significantly to public understanding of government operations.”

🧭 PRO TIPS FOR MAXIMUM SUCCESS

  • Split large requests — “First, send me the 2023 contracts. Then I’ll request 2024.”
  • Request search terms used — “Please include the search terms, databases, and custodians searched.”
  • Cite prior releases — “Similar records released to [Name] on [Date] under FOIA #[Number] — attached.”
  • Submit to multiple offices — Central FOIA + Bureau + Records Custodian.
  • Use MuckRock.com — Tracks deadlines, publishes responses, adds public pressure.
